Dashboard TIF: Digitizing Academic Bureaucracy at UIN Suska Riau

Context

Internship Project

Role

UI/UX Designer

Focus

Complex System Simplification
B2B/Enterprise Dashboard

Academic bureaucracy is often synonymous with rigid and confusing processes. In this internship project, I joined a 9-person team to design the Academic Administration Dashboard for the Informatics Engineering Department at UIN Suska Riau. As a UI/UX Designer, my main responsibility was to overhaul the complicated manual bureaucracy flow—specifically for the Thesis Revision (Murajaah) and Internship modules—into a concise, transparent, and intuitive digital experience.

The Problem

Previously, students and academic staff were trapped in paper-based manual processes that were prone to loss, slow, and opaque. Students struggled to track the status of their internship applications, while administration staff were overwhelmed manually validating hundreds of documents. This lack of status clarity created communication friction and wasted a lot of productive time.

The Approach

  • System Mapping: I dissected the manual bureaucracy SOP and mapped it into a logical digital user flow.
  • Component-Based Design: Designed the UI with a component system (design system) so the dashboard remains consistent and easily scalable in the future.
  • Cross-functional Collaboration: Collaborated closely with engineers to ensure the designs I created were not only ideal for users but also technically feasible to develop within the internship deadline.

The Solution

A modern academic dashboard that prioritizes information clarity. The Thesis Revision and Internship modules are now equipped with visual status trackers, seamless asynchronous application forms, and data tables that are easy for staff to filter. The design is minimalist with a strong typographic hierarchy so users can focus on their tasks without visual distraction.

The Results

This dashboard design was successfully implemented and officially launched (live deployment). The success of this project is clear evidence of solid team collaboration. The biggest lesson I learned was the art of compromise—how to bridge complex institutional business logic with user needs, and guiding the design so it's implemented precisely into code by developers.
