MajorPath: Navigating High School Students to the Right College Major

Context

Bachelor's Thesis

Role

End-to-End UX Researcher
UX Writer
UI/UX Designer

Focus

Decision Support Platform
User-Centered Design (UCD)

Choosing a college major is one of the most crucial life decisions for teenagers, yet ironically, this process is often done without adequate guidance. MajorPath was born as my final year thesis project to answer this challenge. Acting as an end-to-end UX Researcher to UI/UX Designer, I developed a decision support platform that empowers high school students to explore their interests and future prospects through a User-Centered Design (UCD) approach.

The Problem

Many high school students experience confusion, anxiety, and a lack of literacy regarding higher education prospects. They are often trapped in information bias, following friends, or environmental pressure, which leads to choosing the wrong major. The lack of a centralized and digestible information source makes the decision-making process an extremely cognitively exhausting experience.

The Approach

  • Empathy & Synthesis: Processed qualitative data to build User Personas and Customer Journey Maps based on interviews and observations of 15 students at SMA Negeri 6 Pekanbaru.
  • Ideation & Prototyping: Translated research findings into a structured information architecture, followed by designing wireframes up to high-fidelity prototypes.
  • UX Writing: Crafted microcopy that is friendly, motivating, and not patronizing, so teenagers feel guided, not judged.

The Solution

MajorPath is designed as an interactive platform that presents college major information in a structured and comprehensive manner. The interface is made clean with step-by-step navigation that reduces cognitive overload. The exploration feature is designed like a personal assistant that helps filter major options based on interests and factual data, logically guiding users from a point of confusion to a point of confidence.

The Results

This solution was validated by testing the prototype directly with 21 high school students. As a result, MajorPath achieved a System Usability Scale (SUS) score of 72 (above the global usability standard average). This project taught me that good design is not just aesthetics, but how we translate users' psychological data into functional and empathetic decision support systems.
